5. Sichern und Wiederherstellen

Mit «Sichern & Wiederherstellen» exportiert die App Merkliste (inkl. deiner Notizen und Gruppen), Alarme, Favoriten, Portfolio und Einstellungen als JSON-Datei an einen Ort, den du selbst wählst – unter Android über die Android-Dateiauswahl (z. B. «Downloads»), unter iOS über den Dialog der App «Dateien» (z. B. «Auf meinem iPhone» oder ein Speicheranbieter deiner Wahl wie iCloud Drive). Beim Wiederherstellen liest die App nur die Datei, die du dort auswählst. Die App sendet diese Datei nirgendwohin. Wählst du einen Cloud-Speicher, gelten dessen Datenschutzbestimmungen. Ohne Passwort ist die Datei unverschlüsselt (lesbares JSON). Auf Wunsch schützt du die Datei mit einem Passwort: Sie wird dann auf deinem Gerät verschlüsselt (AES-256-GCM, Schlüssel per Argon2id aus dem Passwort und einem zufälligen Salt, der Dateikopf ist mitgeschützt; ältere Sicherungsdateien mit PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 lassen sich weiterhin öffnen); das Passwort wird weder gespeichert noch übertragen, und ohne es lässt sich die Sicherung nicht wiederherstellen. Wie gut die Datei geschützt ist, hängt vom Passwort ab: Für neue Sicherungen sind mindestens 12 Zeichen nötig, und ein leicht zu erratendes Passwort (nur Ziffern, ein wiederholtes Zeichen oder eine häufige Folge wie «12345678») lässt die App nicht zu; ältere Sicherungsdateien öffnest du weiter mit ihrem bisherigen Passwort. Spielst du eine Sicherung ohne Passwort ein, weist die App vorher darauf hin, dass sie nicht verschlüsselt ist; eine eingeschaltete Portfolio-Sperre kann eine solche Datei nicht ausschalten. Sicherungsdateien sind zwischen Android und iOS austauschbar.

Stichtag-Export: Den Wert deines Portfolios an einem Datum speichert die App als CSV-Datei ebenfalls nur an dem Ort, den du im Dateidialog wählst. Die Datei wird nicht übertragen.

6. Benachrichtigungen, Sprachausgabe und Widgets

Benachrichtigungen, Alarme (auch Portfolio-Alarme) und Widgets werden vollständig auf dem Gerät erzeugt; es wird kein Push-Dienst verwendet (unter iOS kein Apple Push Notification Service, unter Android kein Firebase Cloud Messaging). Unter iOS kann die App Alarme als dringende Mitteilungen («Time Sensitive») zustellen, damit sie auch bei aktivem Fokus erscheinen; das lässt sich in den iOS-Einstellungen abschalten. Mit «Beträge verbergen» zeigen Portfolio, Portfolio-Widget und Portfolio-Alarme keine Beträge.

Eigener Alarmton (Android): Wählst du eine eigene Audiodatei als Alarmton, kopiert die App diese Datei auf deinem Gerät in die Mediathek (Ordner «Notifications/CryptoChecker»), damit Android sie für Mitteilungen abspielen kann. Die Datei bleibt auf dem Gerät und wird nicht übertragen; du kannst sie dort jederzeit löschen. Unter iOS stehen nur mitgelieferte Töne zur Wahl.

Für die Sprachausgabe nutzt die App unter Android die auf deinem Gerät installierte Text-to-Speech-Engine (z. B. «Sprachdienste von Google») und unter iOS die Systemstimmen von iOS. Der vorzulesende Text (Kurs, Alarm) wird an diese Engine übergeben; wie sie ihn verarbeitet, richtet sich nach deren Anbieter. Die App selbst speichert oder übermittelt diese Texte nicht.

Die Widgets lesen ihre Daten aus dem lokalen Speicher der App (unter iOS aus dem App-Group-Container, siehe Abschnitt 3) und rufen Kurse bei Bedarf direkt bei den oben genannten Anbietern ab.

Charts from the pair’s exchange: For the chart in the action sheet, the mini chart in the watchlist, the single widget and the 24h change (when the exchange’s price has none), the app first loads the candles from the pair’s own exchange through its public, key-free candle API – the same hosts as for the prices: api.kraken.com (/0/public/OHLC), www.bitstamp.net (/api/v2/ohlc/…), api.bitvavo.com (/v2/…/candles), api.bybit.com (/v5/market/kline), www.okx.com (/api/v5/market/candles), api.exchange.coinbase.com (/products/…/candles), Binance (data-api.binance.vision, api.binance.com, fapi.binance.com, dapi.binance.com), api.binance.us, api-pub.bitfinex.com, api.bitget.com, api.gateio.ws, api.kucoin.com, api.mexc.com, contract.mexc.com, api.huobi.pro, api.gemini.com, api.crypto.com, api.upbit.com, api.woox.io, api.poloniex.com, www.deribit.com, api.hyperliquid.xyz and api.onetrading.com. Only the trading pair’s identifier and the time range are sent. Only if the exchange provides no candles does the app use the previous fallback sources (Binance, Binance.US, Coinbase Exchange) and says so; never for stocks and commodities (TradFi) or DEX pools.

Chart in the action sheet: If a fiat pair (e.g. BTC/CHF) has no candles of its own, the app uses the coin’s USDT candles and converts each candle with the exchange rate of its day. For this it loads the historical daily rates from Frankfurter (the same time series as for the portfolio value history) – only the currency and the date range, at most once per currency every 6 hours.

For the mini chart in the watchlist, the value history of the portfolio widget (only for coins whose hourly prices are still missing, at most once an hour) and the holdings-on-a-date export the app loads public daily or hourly prices from Binance (including its market data mirror, futures and Binance.US) or Coinbase Exchange, and exchange rates from Frankfurter for the conversion. Only the trading pair or currency and the date are requested – never your holdings or portfolio values. Conversion and the valuation of your portfolio are calculated exclusively on your device.

Price from your exchange in the portfolio: If you enter an exchange on a coin’s transactions (e.g. Kraken), the app values that coin at the public spot price of that exchange instead of Binance. For this it loads the exchange’s public pair list and asks it for the coin’s price (e.g. BTC/USD) – only the trading pair is sent, never amount, price or value. For the value history, “7 d”, “1 y” and the portfolio widget it loads the public daily candles of the same pair from that exchange (Binance only as a fallback). If Binance does not list a coin and you follow it in your watchlist on another exchange, the app asks that exchange for the price of that watchlist pair – only the pair the watchlist already requests there. Without an exchange entered, the app only asks Binance for current prices. For past daily closes (value history, tax lines, suggested cost) and missing USDT/USD days the app – when Binance lacks a coin or day – asks Coinbase, Kraken, OKX and Bybit in turn with the coin’s trading pair (e.g. “PEPEUSDT”); amounts are never sent.

Binance COIN-M: If you watch a Binance COIN-M perpetual (e.g. BTCUSD_PERP), the app gets funding rate, open interest and the funding history from dapi.binance.com (/dapi/v1/premiumIndex, /dapi/v1/openInterest, /dapi/v1/fundingRate) – only the contract symbol is sent.

Portfolio alarms in another currency: If a portfolio alarm is in a different currency than the conversion currency (e.g. after switching from CHF to EUR), the app loads that exchange rate from Frankfurter or open.er-api.com as for the conversion line – only the currency, no amounts.

Funding interval: To convert Binance Futures funding rates correctly to 8 hours, the app loads the public list of settlement intervals fapi.binance.com/fapi/v1/fundingInfo at most every 6 hours – the same for everyone, without a symbol.

Thresholds for Bitcoin key figures: If you set a threshold under Settings › Advanced › Market alerts (e.g. Mayer multiple above 2.4), the app checks twice a day on the device with the same public data as in the market tab and notifies you locally. The thresholds stay on the device.

USDT depeg alarm (optional, off by default): If you turn on “Report USDT depeg” under Settings › Alarms, the app additionally requests api.kraken.com/0/public/Ticker?pair=USDTZUSD, api.exchange.coinbase.com/products/USDT-USD/stats and api.binance.com/api/v3/ticker/24hr?symbol=USDCUSDT at most every 5 minutes – the same public interfaces as for prices, without an account or identifiers. If you tap “Open exchange” in the notification, the exchange’s USDC/USDT page opens in the browser or its app; only then does that exchange see your visit.

USDT at market price in the portfolio: As soon as you use the portfolio (turned on, with transactions or watched addresses), the app converts USDT into your currency at its market price in USD. For this it requests – even with the depeg alarm turned off – the same prices as the depeg alarm at most every 5 minutes (api.kraken.com/0/public/Ticker?pair=USDTZUSD, api.exchange.coinbase.com/products/USDT-USD/stats and api.binance.com/api/v3/ticker/24hr?symbol=USDCUSDT) and loads the public daily closes of USDT/USD from Kraken for the value history and the tax export (api.kraken.com/0/public/OHLC?pair=USDTZUSD&interval=1440, at most once every 12 hours). Only this pair is sent – the same request for every user, never holdings or amounts. Without a portfolio and without the depeg alarm, these requests are not made.

Coin logos: For the logos next to the coins (Settings › Advanced › Coin logos, with one switch each for the app, the portfolio and the widgets; by default «In the app» and «In the portfolio» are on, «In widgets» is off) the app fetches, at most once a day, first and in parallel the public symbol list of the Binance website and the public Binance Alpha token list (both www.binance.com) – they provide the names and the recognition of Alpha tokens, independently of everything else –, then CoinGecko’s public list of the roughly 2,500 largest coins (api.coingecko.com, in pages with pauses in between; CoinGecko data is stored for at most 24 hours), and downloads the logos of all these entries directly from the image servers of CoinGecko (coin-images.coingecko.com or assets.coingecko.com) and Binance (bin.bnbstatic.com), several at a time per server – afterwards only new or changed ones. Logos from CoinGecko or Binance are neither mirrored nor shipped with the app; GitHub is not used for logo images. Logos are shown only from the storage on your device (on iOS in the App Group container so the widgets can use them); a single logo of your watchlist is never fetched on demand. The only exception, on the very first start: for the starter selection the app fetches the logos of the five suggested largest coins (e.g. Bitcoin, Ethereum) from the same image servers in advance – the same for every user, regardless of what you choose. Because every installation makes the same requests, neither CoinGecko nor Binance can tell which coins you follow. Only the technically necessary connection data is transmitted. If CoinGecko answers “too many requests”, the app waits for the requested pause (at most one minute) and tries once more; if a source is missing, what is known is kept and the app tries again after a few hours. If a sync fails because there is no internet, the app repeats it as soon as the connection returns or the next time it is opened – with the same requests and a growing pause between attempts (from 5 seconds up to at most 15 minutes), never several at once. Coins that are in none of these lists show their initials. When all switches are off, the app loads no logos. If Settings › Advanced › Token & TradFi › «Incl. Alpha tokens (α)» is on while all logo switches and «Show names» are off, the app loads only the public Binance Alpha token list (www.binance.com) – at most once a day, without the coin list and without images, the same for everyone – to recognise Alpha tokens; if this switch is off as well, it loads none of these lists. For stocks missing from the Binance list, the app downloads the logos of all stock tickers in these pair lists from Binance’s image server (bin.bnbstatic.com), not just those on your watchlist. With «Show names» on, the app gets the names of all US stocks at most once a week directly from Nasdaq’s complete public symbol directory (www.nasdaqtrader.com, two text files) – the same for everyone. They are shown from the device until the downloaded logos and lists are available – nothing is fetched for them. If an image server answers “too many requests”, “forbidden” or “unavailable”, the app pauses that server’s images (as long as requested, otherwise 15 minutes, at most 6 hours) instead of marking them as broken; after a short pause (at most 2 minutes) it fetches the paused images right away.

Watched addresses (optional): If you add a public address to the portfolio, the app queries its balance directly from the provider of that network (no account, no API key): Bitcoin from mempool.space (fallback blockstream.info); Ethereum from ethereum-rpc.publicnode.com (fallback eth.llamarpc.com); the same 0x address on further EVM networks from bsc-rpc.publicnode.com / bsc-dataseed.bnbchain.org (BNB Smart Chain), polygon-bor-rpc.publicnode.com / polygon-rpc.com (Polygon), arbitrum-one-rpc.publicnode.com / arb1.arbitrum.io (Arbitrum), base-rpc.publicnode.com / mainnet.base.org (Base), optimism-rpc.publicnode.com / mainnet.optimism.io (OP Mainnet) and avalanche-c-chain-rpc.publicnode.com / api.avax.network (Avalanche); Solana from api.mainnet.solana.com (fallback solana-rpc.publicnode.com); TRON from tron-rpc.publicnode.com (fallback api.trongrid.io); XRP from xrplcluster.com (fallback s2.ripple.com); Litecoin from litecoinspace.org; TON from toncenter.com (balance and jettons, two requests with a pause in between); Cardano from api.koios.rest (Koios, ADA only). Only the address is sent – never keys or seed phrases. The provider sees the address together with your device’s IP address and can therefore link these holdings to you. With «Count tokens» (can be turned off per address) the app also reads, from the same providers, the balances of a fixed list of common tokens (ERC-20/BEP-20 via balanceOf, TRC-20, SPL tokens by mint, TON jettons; unknown Solana tokens are only counted, not valued). For a 0x address the first query asks all EVM networks listed above (one request per network) and then keeps only the networks with a balance; you can change the selection per address, and deselected networks are no longer queried. For an extended public key (xpub/ypub/zpub): it reveals all addresses of that wallet. The app derives the addresses on your device only and never sends the key itself; only individual derived addresses go to mempool.space or blockstream.info, one request per address – at least 40 on the first scan (until 20 unused addresses in a row per chain), afterwards the used addresses and a few following ones, a full scan at most once a day, with short pauses and at most 400 requests per refresh. Because a provider sees many addresses from the same IP address in quick succession, it can link them to one wallet. The app does not accept private keys (xprv, WIF, Solana keys, XRP seeds) or seed phrases. BNB Beacon Chain addresses (bnb1…) are rejected because that network was shut down in 2024; Dogecoin and Bitcoin Cash addresses too, because there is no free public service without a key whose terms allow use by an app – they are not sent anywhere. Queries happen when you add an address, when you open the portfolio and during the background refresh (for portfolio alarms and the portfolio widget) – at most every 10 minutes per address (a manual refresh can query again after 60 seconds) and only while addresses are added. Addresses, keys and derived addresses are stored on your device. You can set an address to «Show only»; it then does not count towards the portfolio (e.g. if the same coins are already recorded as your own transactions). The app backup contains each address or key with its label, its observed balance history (the last observed value per day, also per token and network) and the settings «Count tokens», selected networks and «Show only»; the same applies to the Android system backup or iOS device backup, but only if «Portfolio in system backup» is on.

5. Backup and restore

With “Backup & restore” the app exports your watchlist (including your notes and groups), alarms, favorites, portfolio and settings as a JSON file to a location you choose yourself – on Android via the Android file picker (e.g. “Downloads”), on iOS via the Files app dialog (e.g. “On My iPhone” or a storage provider of your choice such as iCloud Drive). When restoring, the app only reads the file you pick there. The app does not send this file anywhere. If you choose a cloud storage, its privacy terms apply. Without a password the file is not encrypted (readable JSON). You can optionally protect the file with a password: it is then encrypted on your device (AES-256-GCM, key derived from the password and a random salt with Argon2id, the file header is authenticated as well; older backup files using PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 can still be opened); the password is neither stored nor transmitted, and without it the backup cannot be restored. How well the file is protected depends on the password: new backups need at least 12 characters, and the app does not accept an easy-to-guess password (only digits, one repeated character or a common sequence such as “12345678”); older backup files still open with their previous password. If you restore a backup without a password, the app first points out that it is not encrypted; such a file cannot turn off an active portfolio lock. Backup files can be exchanged between Android and iOS.

Cut-off export: The value of your portfolio on a date is saved as a CSV file only to the location you choose in the file dialog. The file is not transmitted.

The text to be spoken (price, alarm) is handed to that engine; how it processes the text depends on its provider. The app itself neither stores nor transmits these texts.

Widgets read their data from the app’s local storage (on iOS from the App Group container, see section 3) and fetch prices directly from the providers listed above when needed.
